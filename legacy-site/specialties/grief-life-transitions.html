Loss doesn’t follow a timeline. And it deserves more than time.

Grief is not just about death. It’s about any significant loss — a relationship, a version of yourself, a life you thought you’d have, a future that didn’t materialize. All of it counts. And all of it can be worked with.

Grief is more complicated than people think it will be.

Maybe you lost someone and the grief isn’t moving the way people said it would. You’re not progressing through stages. You’re just stuck, or cycling, or getting ambushed at unexpected moments.

Maybe it’s not a death — it’s a divorce, a diagnosis, a job that defined you, a friendship that ended, a child leaving home, a move that cost you more than you expected. Grief doesn’t require a grave.

Maybe the hardest part is that you feel like you shouldn’t still be struggling. That you should be “over it” by now. That feeling is one of the cruelest things grief does — it convinces you that how you feel is wrong.

Processing grief at the level where it actually lives.

Grief isn’t just an emotional experience. It’s a body experience. The loss lives in the nervous system, in muscle memory, in the automatic reach for someone who is no longer there.

EMDR can work with the specific memories, moments, and stuck points that keep grief from moving — the last conversation you wish had gone differently, the moment you found out, the things you never said. Processing these doesn’t erase the loss. It allows the loss to exist without you being trapped inside it.

The goal isn’t to stop missing them. It’s to be able to miss them without it pulling you under every time.

Your grief deserves real support.

The free 15-minute call is where you start. Bring whatever you’re carrying — it all belongs here.
