It’s more complicated than people think.

Leaving — or wanting to leave — is not simple. Love and fear and confusion exist at the same time. That doesn’t mean something is wrong with you. It means you’re human, and the relationship was complicated.

You might be processing something that happened in the past. Or navigating a situation that is still ongoing. Either way, having a safe place to think clearly — where no one will judge your choices or pressure you toward a particular decision — is what therapy can offer.

Telehealth sessions offer an additional layer of privacy. No shared waiting room. No one who knows you seeing your car in the parking lot. Your sessions are yours, completely.

Processing without re-traumatization.

EMDR allows you to process what happened without having to narrate every detail out loud. For many survivors of domestic violence and abuse, this is a significant relief.

The work focuses on specific incidents, the beliefs they left behind (“I deserved it,” “I should have known,” “I am the problem”), and the body’s stored response to ongoing threat. Processing these at the level of the nervous system — not just through talking — is what allows real change.

The pace is entirely yours. Nothing happens before you are ready. Lianne’s first priority is your sense of safety in the sessions themselves.

You don’t have to figure it all out before reaching out.

The free consultation call is confidential and carries no obligation. You can ask questions, share as little or as much as you want, and decide from there.
