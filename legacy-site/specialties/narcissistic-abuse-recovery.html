You’re not crazy. And you’re not the problem.

One of the most disorienting things about recovering from a relationship with a narcissist is that you often don’t fully understand what happened until you’re out — and even then, you question yourself. That questioning is part of what the relationship did to you. Therapy can help you find your footing again.

The specific patterns that bring people here.

You might be out of the relationship and still hear them in your head — second-guessing your own perceptions, catching yourself wondering if you were the unreasonable one.

You might be hypervigilant in ways that feel out of proportion to your current situation — scanning for threats in ordinary interactions, braced for criticism, exhausted by the level of alertness you’re maintaining.

You might have lost your sense of who you are. A long relationship with a narcissist often involves a slow erosion of your preferences, your confidence, your trust in your own judgment. Getting that back is real work.

You might not even be sure if what you experienced qualifies as abuse. The answer is: if it left these marks, it counts.

Processing specific incidents changes the overall pattern.

Narcissistic abuse often involves dozens — sometimes hundreds — of specific incidents that together created the damage. The moment your reality was dismissed. The conversation that left you feeling like you were losing your mind.

EMDR processes these incidents specifically. Not all of them — the brain generalizes, and processing a handful of representative experiences often shifts the whole constellation.

The goal isn’t to forget what happened. It’s for those memories to lose their charge — so they’re something you can look back on clearly, rather than something that pulls you under when you’re just trying to live your life.

What becomes possible after this work.

Your own perceptions stop feeling suspect. The voice that second-guesses everything starts to quiet. You begin to trust yourself again — what you feel, what you observe, what you want.

The hypervigilance doesn’t disappear overnight. But it starts to calibrate to the actual level of threat in your current environment, rather than the one you survived.

You grieve what you lost — including the version of the relationship you thought you were in. That grief is real and it deserves space. And over time, you find your way back to yourself.
