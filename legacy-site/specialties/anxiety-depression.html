Anxiety and depression look different for different people.

Anxiety

For some people it’s loud — constant worry, racing thoughts, catastrophic scenarios running on a loop. For others it’s quieter — a baseline unease, a body that won’t fully relax, a readiness for something bad to happen that never quite goes away. It might look like avoiding things that trigger it, which works short-term and slowly makes the world smaller.

Depression

Depression doesn’t always look like sadness. Sometimes it looks like flatness. The absence of things that used to matter. Going through motions. A tiredness that sleep doesn’t fix. It might look like irritability, not tears. Or functioning just fine on the outside while feeling completely empty on the inside.

For many people, anxiety and depression arrive together, each feeding the other in a loop that’s exhausting to live inside.

Why treating the root changes the symptoms.

Anxiety and depression are rarely random. They usually connect to something — a history, specific experiences, beliefs that formed early and have been running quietly ever since.

Talk therapy can help you understand those connections. EMDR helps you actually process them — so the belief “I am fundamentally unsafe” or “I am not enough” stops triggering the same physical and emotional response every time.

When the root changes, the symptoms often follow in ways that feel genuinely different from just managing them better.
