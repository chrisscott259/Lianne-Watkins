The questions most people have before they reach out.

Honest answers to the questions Lianne hears most — about how the process works, what her credentials mean, what insurance covers, and what to expect.

How do I know if therapy is right for me?

If something feels stuck — a pattern you keep repeating, emotions that feel disproportionate, pain that won’t move — therapy is probably worth exploring. You don’t need to be in crisis to benefit. You just need to want something to change.

What’s the first step?

Book a free 15-minute consultation call. It costs nothing, requires no paperwork, and carries no obligation. It’s just a conversation to see if working together makes sense.

What if I’ve tried therapy before and it didn’t help?

This is one of the most common things Lianne hears. There are several reasons therapy sometimes doesn’t help: a poor fit with the therapist, an approach that didn’t match the problem, or a point in life when you weren’t ready. None of these mean therapy can’t help you.

Do I need a diagnosis or referral to start therapy?

No. You don’t need a referral from a doctor, and you don’t need a diagnosis. You can reach out directly.

How does telehealth therapy work?

Sessions take place via secure video — HIPAA-compliant and private. You’ll receive a link before each session. All you need is a quiet space, a device with a camera, and a reliable internet connection.

Is telehealth as effective as in-person therapy?

Research consistently shows that telehealth therapy produces outcomes equivalent to in-person sessions for most conditions, including trauma and anxiety. Many clients find it easier to open up from their own space.

Can I do therapy from anywhere in California?

Yes. As long as you are physically located in California during sessions, Lianne can see you. She cannot see clients located outside California at the time of the session.

What do I need for a telehealth session?

A quiet, private space. A device with a working camera and microphone. A stable internet connection. Headphones can help with privacy and sound quality.

What does AMFT mean?

AMFT stands for Associate Marriage and Family Therapist. It is the California license designation for therapists who have completed graduate training and are completing the supervised clinical hours required for full MFT licensure. Lianne also holds the APCC credential (Associate Professional Clinical Counselor), which follows the same supervised-hours model.

What is supervised practice?

California requires all associate therapists to work under the oversight of a licensed clinician until they have completed sufficient supervised hours and passed their licensing exams. This is a standard and required part of the path to licensure — it means Lianne is actively accountable to a licensed supervisor who reviews her work.

Does supervision affect my privacy?

Her supervisor is also bound by confidentiality requirements. Case consultation happens at a clinical level without unnecessary identifying detail. Your identity and the specifics of your situation are protected.

Still have questions?

The consultation call is the right place to ask them. It’s free, it’s short, and Lianne will give you straight answers.
